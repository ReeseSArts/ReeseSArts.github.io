About Our Website

The Problem → Promise pattern describes communication that begins by identifying a problem, or need that the reader has, whether they know it in that moment or not, and then presenting the promise that the website, product, or service can solve it. Instead of immediately promoting a product or idea, the writer first demonstrates an understanding of the reader's situation in order to build trust and create a stronger connection. This pattern is widely used in websites, advertisements, and digital marketing because it naturally guides readers through a storytelling process. A successful Problem → Promise structure helps visitors feel understood and gives them a reason to continue exploring the website rather than leaving immediately.

In order to achieve this connection, there is a way of storytelling within your website that captures a visitors attention, and a lot of it comes from a compelling header. Like said in chapter 3.2, 8/10 people will read your header, but if it does not attract them, 6 out of those 8 will move on. This is true with images as well, as your brain can process a photo in milliseconds, allowing images to communicate emotions and ideas faster than text alone. While they seem like small things, without these structural components a website becomes difficult to navigate, less engaging, and far less successful at communicating its message or encouraging visitors to take action.
